# Release 1.21.0: Momentum flight and lit ships

The first piece of the v2 overhaul: the ship now flies with momentum, phones steer by dragging, and the player ship and Fighter are rebuilt as lit 3D-rendered craft. The ten-level campaign, the other enemies, and the backgrounds are unchanged until later v2 phases.

## Flying

- The ship winds up when you start moving and glides about a third of a second to rest instead of stopping dead. Handling is the same at 30, 60, and 120 fps. The cap stays at 480 px/s and diagonals stay normalized.
- The ship banks visibly into turns, using rendered bank frames rather than flat rotation.
- On phones and tablets, drag anywhere: the ship follows your finger's movement rather than jumping to it, and the main gun fires automatically. Lifting and touching again, respawning, rotating the device, or switching to a keyboard never makes the ship jump. Only a real touch hands control back to touch, so trackpad clicks on hybrid devices can't trigger autofire.
- The virtual joystick is gone.

## Ships and light

- The player interceptor and the Fighter are built as 3D models in code and rendered into sprite sheets with normal maps (`bun run art:shipyard`). Logical sizes and collision bodies are unchanged.
- Explosions cast warm light onto nearby ships, the player's engines glow on its hull, and bloom picks out engines and fire.
- Quality tiers budget the cost:

| Tier | Lighting |
| --- | --- |
| Low | Pre-lit look only, no dynamic lights or bloom |
| Standard | 4 lights, soft bloom |
| High | 8 lights, full bloom |
| Auto | Phones start on Standard's budget; sheds bloom, then lights, under frame pressure |

- Pausing freezes light fades with the other gameplay clocks. Respawn and level transitions let them finish.

## Verification

Both TypeScript compilers, lint, formatting, all unit tests (129 files), level validation, unused-code and architecture checks, build and bundle limits, and the update-cost performance gate pass. Desktop and touch browser gates pass in CI, including new checks for bank frames and real rendered lighting: Standard visibly brightens a ship near a light pulse, and Low does not. An independent Sol review approved the final behavior across five rounds. Review-bot findings were fixed with tests: lights freezing during respawn, stale drag anchors, and touch autofire on hybrid devices.

Real-phone smoothness with dynamic lighting is not yet measured. CI renders WebGL in software at about 1 fps, so the browser gates cannot judge GPU cost. The v2 playtest checklist (`docs/qa/v2-phase-0-playtest.md`) covers it, and momentum feel will be tuned from that playtest.
