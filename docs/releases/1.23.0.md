# Release 1.23.0: The shipyard

A graphics overhaul across the whole game: every hull is a lit 3D model, combat effects are layered and luminous, space has depth, and the command deck comes alive. Gameplay, hitboxes, flight feel and encounter timing are unchanged.

## Ships

- Every hull is a code-built 3D model rendered into normal-mapped sprites: the player (with nine banking frames), the helper wing, all eleven enemy families, the picket turret, the sower mine and four asteroid shapes. Each enemy keeps its learned silhouette and colour.
- Six boss capital ships, one per attack style: a gun-studded barrage battleship, a shield-walled bulwark fortress, a twin-deck carrier with hangar bays, a forward-swept pursuit predator, a spinning maelstrom disc and a hammer-headed pressure siege platform.
- Explosions, engine lights and pulses light the hulls per pixel, so fights visibly glint off nearby armour.

## Effects

- Explosions layer a white-hot flash and lens flare, a billowing fireball that cools from white through orange to red, sparks and drifting embers in the ship's colour, a shockwave ring shaped to the hull, lingering smoke and, for heavy ships, debris.
- Your shots are plasma lances with a white core; hostile fire is a magenta plasma orb with a dark rim, so it stays readable over bright nebulae. Turret bolts burn amber. Laser beams have a smooth falloff whose bright core matches exactly where they hurt.
- Your ship burns a flickering twin engine flame that stretches when you thrust forward. Pickups are glossy glass orbs.

## Space

- A three-layer starfield in real stellar colours: dense faint stars far away, brighter haloed stars in the middle, and a few bright diffraction-spiked stars close by, each drifting at its own speed.
- A vignette frames the playfield without dimming the centre lane.

## Command deck

- The hero ship is lit by an orbiting key light that rolls highlights across its plates, and idles over a launch pad with scan rings and live engine exhaust.
- A planet horizon rises behind the run tiles and shooting stars cross the sky. Reduced motion keeps the scene still.

## Under the hood

- One art pipeline: `bun run art:shipyard` renders every sheet from code (465 KB total). The flat neon drawings, the image-based ship pipeline and the development-only legacy backgrounds are gone, about 6,400 lines in total.
- Sheet loading now also rejects a wrong image that happens to slice into correctly sized frames.

## Verification

Both TypeScript compilers, lint, formatting, unit tests, level validation, unused-code and architecture checks, build and bundle limits pass locally, as do the desktop and touch browser suites. The menu and gameplay were reviewed in GPU-rendered screenshots at desktop and phone sizes on high and low quality.
